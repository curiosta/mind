The spellbook

Five spells. One method.

Science from Oakley & Sejnowski, Learning How to Learn and A Mind for Numbers, and Hutchinson, Concept Development Studies in Chemistry.

1 · The Patronus

A real memory, not a borrowed one. Recall one specific memory a day — smells, words, hands. Write it. Sleep on it. Oakley: memory consolidates in sleep; rereading is an illusion of competence. The test: can someone else picture it from your telling?

2 · Occlumency

Name the feeling before you act. Anticipating an unpleasant task lights up pain regions; the pain fades once you start. A claim that arrives as a finished fact, with no reasoning shown, is itself the signal.

3 · Legilimency

Notice what people repeat and what they avoid. The Einstellung effect: focused mode keeps returning to the same frame. Step away. Most of us are inductive learners — seeing where a claim came from makes it acceptable, or not.

4 · Priori Incantatem

Primary source, not the article that cites it. Reverse-image search. Who collected the statistic, when, and why? Chunk it into one sentence before you pass it on.

5 · The Foundation

Hutchinson's method, and the meta-spell. Foundation, questions, observations, deductions, model — in that order. Models are never proven. If you can only state the model, you accepted the conclusion. You did not build it.
