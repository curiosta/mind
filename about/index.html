About

A Curiosta brand.

Plain words. The frame is ours, and the science is cited. Sources checked 11 Oct 2026.

mind is an editorial site in the Curiosta family, alongside learn, kinetic, buildwith and founder. It has two jobs: how to learn when answers are free, and how not to be fooled by a machine or a person.

The spell names (Patronus, Occlumency, Legilimency, Priori Incantatem, plus our own addition, the Foundation) are a memory aid. They're not a claim about fiction, and this isn't a course. The drills under them come from two public sources:

  1. Barbara Oakley and Terrence Sejnowski: the Coursera course Learning How to Learn, and Oakley's book A Mind for Numbers. Focused and diffuse modes, chunking, illusions of competence, retrieval, sleep.
  2. John S. Hutchinson, Concept Development Studies in Chemistry, Rice University, free and open. Foundation, questions, observations, deductions, model. In his words, it aims to "redirect the focus of learning."

What's ours is the mapping, the order of the drills, the experiments, and the decision to write them as something you can cast on a weekday. The five ideas underneath are defined on the ideas page: learning and defence as one skill, the barrier moving from access to judgment, the dark arts as old tricks made cheap, the closed-tool rule, and Hutchinson's build-last order. The first-person account of why these names and not others is on the spells page.

How the experiments work

Every page asks you to do something small before it explains anything. That's Hutchinson's order applied to the site itself: observation first, model last. All the examples (the voice note, the school, Riverton's trees, the five messages) are invented and labelled that way. None of them is a report about a real person or place.

There's no tracking and no analytics, and nothing you type is sent anywhere. The only thing kept is the Patronus memory, and only if you press "Keep it here". It stays in your own browser's storage until you delete it or clear your browser.
