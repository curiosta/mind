About

A Curiosta brand.

Plain words. The frame is ours, and the science is cited. Helplines and case-file sources checked 24 Oct 2026.

mind is an editorial site in the Curiosta family, alongside learn, kinetic, buildwith and founder. It has two jobs: how to learn when answers are free, and how not to be fooled by a machine or a person.

The spell names (Patronus, Occlumency, Legilimency, Priori Incantatem, plus our own addition, the Foundation) are a memory aid. They're not a claim about fiction, and this isn't a course. The drills under them come from two public sources:

  1. Barbara Oakley and Terrence Sejnowski: the Coursera course Learning How to Learn, and Oakley's book A Mind for Numbers. Focused and diffuse modes, chunking, illusions of competence, retrieval, sleep.
  2. John S. Hutchinson, Concept Development Studies in Chemistry, Rice University, free and open. Foundation, questions, observations, deductions, model. In his words, it aims to "redirect the focus of learning."

What's ours is the mapping, the order of the drills, the experiments, and the decision to write them as something you can cast on a weekday. The five ideas underneath are defined on the ideas page, our "how we think" page: learning and defence as one skill, the barrier moving from access to judgment, the dark arts as old tricks made cheap, the closed-tool rule, and Hutchinson's build-last order. The first-person account of why these names and not others is on the spells page.

How the experiments work

Every page asks you to do something small before it explains anything. That's Hutchinson's order applied to the site itself: observation first, model last. Two kinds of example appear on the site, and you can always tell which. Invented examples (the voice note, the school, Riverton's trees, the five messages) are labelled as invented, and none of them is a report about a real person or place. Real studies and official records are named, with authors, year and a link, and sit in the case files. We never attach an invented quote or number to a real person or organisation.

How we check, and what we are not

Claims in the case files carry a tier (primary, reputed media, or plausible lead), a reason for the tier, the doubts we still have, and questions for you to ponder. The rules, including how we upgrade, drop and correct things, are on how we check. The stance is detective, not soothsayer: we follow clues and say where they lean.

mind is not a clinic, a helpline or a legal or financial adviser. If you need to talk to someone, Get help now lists free lines, each from the organisation's own page with the date we checked it.

Sources and credits

The drills draw on Oakley and Sejnowski and on Hutchinson, above. Specific statements on the site rest on these papers, which we opened by DOI:

  • Cowan (2001), Behavioral and Brain Sciences, for the point on /learn/ that working memory holds only a handful of items (about four chunks).
  • Cepeda et al. (2006), Psychological Bulletin, on spaced practice, for the spacing point on /learn/.
  • The studies in each case file, listed at the foot of that file.

We also watched Sahar Yousef's conversation with Raj Shamani (YouTube, 4Vz6L8B73i4) while planning. It is not behind any exercise that is live today. When an exercise draws on it, that exercise will credit it, with timestamps, where it is used.

There's no tracking and no analytics, and nothing you type into the experiments is sent anywhere. The only thing kept is the Patronus memory, and only if you press "Keep it here". It stays in your own browser's storage until you delete it or clear your browser.

The one third-party embed is comments. The content pages (not the home page and not Get help now) end with a "Discuss this page" section run by giscus, which stores comments as GitHub Discussions on the site's public repository. A small script loads from giscus.app with those pages, and the comment box itself only loads when you scroll to it. Nothing from giscus runs on the home page. Posting needs a GitHub account, and what you post is public under GitHub's terms. Please don't post personal crises or contact details. Use the Get help now page instead.
