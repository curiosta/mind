Module · Money

The pressure to pay now.

Scam calls, "guaranteed" tips, a uniform on a video call. This module is about how the push works and how to slow it down. It's education about process. It isn't financial advice, and it has no tips.

Three rules first

Before anything else on this page.

  1. No government agency investigates you by phone or video call. The Prime Minister said so in Mann Ki Baat on 27 Oct 2024 (PIB). A "digital arrest" is a scam.
  2. Never share an OTP, PIN or password. Under RBI's 2017 circular, if a loss comes from you sharing payment credentials, you bear it until you report it to the bank.
  3. Lost money? Report it at once on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, then tell your bank. Who to call →

This module explains how pressure works. It does not tell you what to buy, sell or invest in.

01 · Challenge

Fifteen questions. Answer first, read later.

Three questions for each of the five chapters below. Pick an answer for each, then score yourself. Every answer links to its chapter.

A self-check, not a certification

  1. 1. Invented scenario: a video call from a man in uniform says he is a "CBI officer". Your Aadhaar is "linked to a parcel of drugs". Stay on camera and move your savings to a "verification account" within 30 minutes. What is the caller's strongest tool?
    Answer

    The 30-minute clock. Stajano and Wilson (2011) list "Time" among seven principles scammers use: hurry you, and you stop checking. The evidence and the uniform are props for the clock. Chapter: Pause before you act →

  2. 2. Can a government agency in India investigate you over a phone or video call?
    Answer

    No. The Prime Minister said in Mann Ki Baat that no government agency does this. PIB's release on Mann Ki Baat, 27 Oct 2024, reports that the Prime Minister reminded citizens that no government agency conducts investigations via phone or video calls, and asked people to report at 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in. Chapter: Pause before you act →

  3. 3. Money left your account in a transaction you did not make, through a breach elsewhere in the system. Under RBI's circular of 6 Jul 2017, you have zero liability if you tell your bank within how many working days?
    Answer

    3. RBI circular DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18: zero liability for a third-party breach reported within 3 working days. From 4 to 7 working days your liability is capped by account type. After that, the bank's board-approved policy applies. Chapter: Pause before you act →

  4. 4. Martel, Pennycook and Rand (2020) asked some people to rely on their emotions while judging news posts. What happened?
    Answer

    They believed fake posts more, but not real ones. In four experiments (total N = 3,884), inducing reliance on emotion raised belief in fake but not real news, compared with a control or with relying on reason. Chapter: Name the feeling →

  5. 5. Invented message: "Your son is with the police. Pay ₹50,000 now and don't tell anyone, or he goes to jail tonight." Which two levers is it pulling?
    Answer

    Fear and secrecy. Fear crowds out checking. Secrecy cuts you off from the one person who could check for you. This breakdown is our reading of the tactic, in the same way as the voice-note lab on /concepts/. Chapter: Name the feeling →

  6. 6. In Lieberman and colleagues' 2007 brain-imaging study, what did putting a feeling into words do?
    Answer

    Lowered the response of the amygdala, a part of the brain involved in threat. That is a brain-level result. No study we found shows that naming a feeling makes people less likely to pay a scammer. Naming buys a pause. The call back protects you. Chapter: Name the feeling →

  7. 7. A caller says he is from your bank and asks you to confirm a transaction. What is the safest check?
    Answer

    Hang up and call the number printed on your card or on the bank's own website. Trace the source through a channel you already had. Anything the caller supplies, including an ID or a call-back number, comes from the caller. Chapter: Trace the source →

  8. 8. A Telegram channel promises "guaranteed 3% a week" on share tips. Where can you check whether a person or firm is registered with SEBI?
    Answer

    SEBI's list of recognised intermediaries on sebi.gov.in. sebi.gov.in has a "Recognised Intermediaries" section. SEBI's circular of 22 Oct 2024 also says regulated entities must not associate with people who give securities advice without being registered, or who claim returns without SEBI's permission. Chapter: Trace the source →

  9. 9. You got a suspected fraud call but lost no money. Where does the Department of Telecommunications ask you to report the call?
    Answer

    Chakshu, on the Sanchar Saathi portal. Chakshu (sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/) takes reports of suspected fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages received within the last 30 days. If you already lost money, the same page says to report at 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in. Chapter: Trace the source →

  10. 10. Friestad and Wright's Persuasion Knowledge Model (1994) says that people...
    Answer

    Learn what persuaders want and how they work, and use that knowledge to cope. Their model is about persuasion knowledge: what you know about an agent's goals and tactics. Asking "who benefits?" puts that knowledge to work. The model is theory, not a trial showing the question protects you. Chapter: Ask who benefits →

  11. 11. Invented: a "free" trading webinar ends with an offer to join a paid VIP group for tips. Who is sure to benefit if you join?
    Answer

    The host, from your joining fee. Whatever the tips turn out to be, the fee is the one payment certain to happen. Ask who gets paid, how much, and when, before you weigh the promise. Chapter: Ask who benefits →

  12. 12. Stajano and Wilson's "Need and Greed" principle says scammers work by...
    Answer

    Finding out what you want, then offering it. Their seven principles come from studying real scams (Communications of the ACM, 2011). A promise built around exactly what you want is a reason to ask who benefits. Chapter: Ask who benefits →

  13. 13. From FY2023-24 to FY2025-26, more than ₹56,087 crore was reported lost to cyber fraud on the national portal, and more than ₹206 crore was refunded. Roughly how much came back for every ₹100 reported?
    Answer

    About 37 paise. 206 ÷ 56,087 ≈ 0.37%, so about 37 paise per ₹100. This is our arithmetic on the Ministry of Home Affairs' Rajya Sabha reply (PIB, 22 Jul 2026). The ₹11,158 crore "saved" is a different measure over a different period, so don't add it in. Chapter: Compare it to something →

  14. 14. Invented numbers: an app flags 9 in 10 scam calls, and wrongly flags 1 in 100 genuine calls. 1 in 100 of your calls is a scam. Out of 10,000 calls, roughly what share of the flagged calls are scams?
    Answer

    About half. Count it out: 100 scam calls, 90 flagged. 9,900 genuine calls, 99 flagged. So 90 of 189 flags are scams, about half. Forgetting how rare scams are (the base rate) is what Bar-Hillel (1980) studied. Chapter: Compare it to something →

  15. 15. Gigerenzer and Hoffrage (1995) gave people probability problems in two forms. When did more of them reason correctly?
    Answer

    With natural counts, like "8 out of 1,000". With frequency formats, people with no training solved up to 50% of the problems the Bayesian way. That is why the question above counts calls instead of using percentages. Chapter: Compare it to something →

    02 · The science

    Five chapters, five of the site's concepts.

    Each chapter takes one concept into money. Each says who found it, how, and where the evidence stops.

    Chapter · Name the feeling

    Fear and secrecy do the selling.

    Money scams rarely argue. They frighten (an arrest, a son in custody, an account about to be frozen) and they isolate ("don't tell anyone"). Fear crowds out checking. Secrecy removes the person who would have checked for you.

    Why it works

    In experiments, people who were asked to rely on their emotions believed more fake news, but not more real news. Putting a feeling into a plain word turns its volume down, at least in the brain and body.

    1. Martel, Pennycook and Rand (2020), Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. Four experiments, N = 3,884. Relying on emotion raised belief in fake but not real news. doi:10.1186/s41235-020-00252-3Tier 1
    2. Lieberman and colleagues (2007), Psychological Science. Brain imaging. Labelling feelings lowered the amygdala's response. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.xTier 1

    Limits

    • These are studies of news and lab feelings, not of payments. We found no study showing that naming a feeling stops people paying a scammer. Naming buys a pause. The call back on your own number protects you.

    Chapter · Trace the source

    Check through a door you already had.

    Anything the caller gives you (an ID, a "verification" number, a link, a screenshot of a certificate) comes from the caller. The check has to go through a channel you found yourself.

    • A "bank" call: hang up. Call the number printed on your card or on the bank's own website.
    • A tip seller: look the person or firm up on SEBI's own list of recognised intermediaries. Since SEBI's circular of 22 Oct 2024, regulated entities must not associate with people who give securities advice without being registered or permitted, or who claim returns without SEBI's permission.
    • A suspected fraud call, no money lost: report it on Chakshu (Department of Telecommunications), if it reached you in the last 30 days.

    Sources: SEBI circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-1/P/CIR/2024/143, 22 Oct 2024 (Tier 1, opened 30 Oct 2026). DoT Chakshu page (Tier 1, opened 30 Oct 2026). The tree-chain drill on /concepts/ shows how a claim grows hop by hop.

    This idea on /concepts/ →

    Chapter · Pause before you act

    The clock is the first thing to doubt.

    A 30-minute deadline, "stay on the call", "the account freezes at 6". Scammers hurry you because a hurried person doesn't check. The official three-step version is in the Prime Minister's Mann Ki Baat of 27 Oct 2024: "Ruko, Socho, Action Lo" (stop, think, then act) (PIB).

    Why it works

    People answering under time pressure believed more false headlines than when they could think again. A small prompt to think about accuracy cut what people said they'd share.

    1. Stajano and Wilson (2011), Communications of the ACM. "Time" is one of seven principles scammers exploit, from studying real scams. doi:10.1145/1897852.1897872Tier 1 · field observation
    2. Bago, Rand and Pennycook (2020), Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. N = 1,635. Fast answers under pressure, then slow ones. doi:10.1037/xge0000729Tier 1
    3. Pennycook and Rand (2022), Nature Communications. 20 experiments, N = 26,863. Accuracy prompts cut sharing intentions for false headlines by about 10%. doi:10.1038/s41467-022-30073-5Tier 1 · same group

    ✋ Feel the number

    About 10% lower means that of every 100 false headlines people said they'd share, about 90 still would be. A pause helps. It isn't a shield. Our arithmetic. These are sharing intentions about headlines, not scam payments.

    Limits

    • Accuracy prompts shrink in replications and at scale (see Pause on /concepts/). Carrying headline studies over to scam calls is our inference.

    Chapter · Ask who benefits

    The fee is a fact. The return is a promise.

    For any offer (a webinar, a VIP group, a "verification account"), list the payments that are certain to happen if you say yes, and who receives each one. Then weigh the promise.

    1. Friestad and Wright (1994), Journal of Consumer Research. The Persuasion Knowledge Model: people learn persuaders' goals and tactics, and use that knowledge to cope. doi:10.1086/209380Tier 1 · theory
    2. Stajano and Wilson (2011). "Need and Greed": find out what the victim wants, then offer it. doi:10.1145/1897852.1897872Tier 1 · field observation

    Limits

    • This is theory and field observation, not a trial. We label it "theory + review".

    Chapter · Compare it to something

    Big compared with what?

    ₹206 crore refunded sounds large. Next to ₹56,087 crore reported over the same three years, it's about 37 paise in every ₹100. The ₹11,158 crore "saved" is a different measure, over a longer period, so it can't be compared directly with either.

    ✋ Feel the number

    37 paise in ₹100

    Our arithmetic on the Ministry of Home Affairs' figures (PIB, 22 Jul 2026): 206 ÷ 56,087 ≈ 0.37%. The figures are the Ministry's, and we found no independent audit of them.

    1. Gigerenzer and Hoffrage (1995), Psychological Review. Counts ("8 out of 1,000") beat percentages for reasoning. doi:10.1037/0033-295X.102.4.684Tier 1
    2. Bar-Hillel (1980), Acta Psychologica. The base-rate fallacy. doi:10.1016/0001-6918(80)90046-3Tier 1

    03 · Lab

    Two answers.

    Six invented messages. First pass: 5 seconds each. Second pass: as long as you like. Then see how many answers you changed. It's a small model of Bago and colleagues' method (fast answers, then slow ones), not a test of you.

    Invented messages · nothing is sent anywhere

    1. "Your parcel is held at customs. Pay ₹49 at this link within 2 hours or it will be returned."

      Answer

      Scam pattern: a small fee, a link, a deadline.

    2. Inside your bank's own app, which you opened yourself: "Your October statement is ready."

      Answer

      Fine: you opened the channel yourself, and it asks for nothing.

    3. A video call from a man in uniform: "CBI. Your Aadhaar is linked to a drugs parcel. Stay on camera."

      Answer

      Scam: no government agency investigates by video call (PIB, 27 Oct 2024).

    4. "Earn ₹5,000 a day liking videos. Pay a ₹1,000 joining fee to start."

      Answer

      Scam pattern: you pay to be paid. Ask who benefits.

    5. An email from your HR team: "Form 16 is available on the usual payroll portal." No link, no attachment.

      Answer

      Fine: it points to a place you already use and asks for nothing.

    6. "Your electricity will be cut tonight at 9:30. Call this number now to update your bill."

      Answer

      Scam pattern: a deadline and a number supplied by the message.

    What changed

      Labelled a model: six invented messages are not a study. The study it borrows from is Bago, Rand and Pennycook (2020), doi:10.1037/xge0000729.

      04 · The ladder

      What to do, in order.

      1. Pause. End the call or close the chat. The deadline is part of the message. Concept you're using: Pause
      2. Check. Call back on a number you found yourself: the one on your card, the bank's site, or a relative's saved number. Concept you're using: Trace the source
      3. Report. If money has gone, call 1930 or file on cybercrime.gov.in as soon as you can. Concept you're using: Name the feeling, so embarrassment doesn't delay you
      4. Tell your bank. In writing, quickly. RBI's 2017 circular sets the windows: zero liability within 3 working days where the fault lies elsewhere in the system; capped liability from 4 to 7 working days. Concept you're using: Compare: days matter
      5. Report the call. Even with no money lost, report a suspected fraud call or message on Chakshu. Concept you're using: Who benefits
      6. Help someone. Tell one person, especially an older relative, what happened and what you did. Concept you're using: Anchor memory

      05 · Who to call

      Each from its own official page.

      • Money lost to cyber fraud: 1930, or cybercrime.gov.in. Run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), Ministry of Home Affairs (PIB, 22 Jul 2026).
      • Your bank: only on the number printed on your card or on the bank's own website. RBI's rules on unauthorised transactions: circular of 6 Jul 2017.
      • Is this adviser registered? SEBI's recognised intermediaries.
      • A suspected fraud call, SMS or WhatsApp: Chakshu on Sanchar Saathi, Department of Telecommunications.
      • In danger now: 112. For distress after a scam: Get help now lists free lines.

      Official pages opened 30 Oct 2026 (IST). The cybercrime.gov.in site itself didn't open for our checker that day; its address and the 1930 number are confirmed in the PIB releases above.
