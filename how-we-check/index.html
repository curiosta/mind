How we check

Detective, not soothsayer.

We follow clues, say where they point, and say what we don't know. We don't predict, and we don't claim more certainty than the sources give us.

01 · The stance

What that means in practice.

  • A claim comes with its clues, for and against, and the doubts we still have.
  • We say where the clues lean. That is a leaning, not a verdict, and it changes if the clues do.
  • Less certain material may appear, but only labelled, with the reason it's there and questions for you to ponder.
  • Every claim has a source you can open, and a date we last opened it.

02 · The three tiers

A tier describes the source, not whether the claim is true.

Tier 1 · Primary

We opened the source

A peer-reviewed paper, an official document, a press release, or the organisation's own page, opened by us on the stated date. Even a Tier 1 source can be wrong or narrow. The card says what it can't tell us.

Tier 2 · Reputed media

A reputable outlet, or a primary we couldn't open

A reputed news outlet reporting on a primary source, or a primary source we know exists but couldn't open ourselves. For anything about health, money or safety, a Tier 2 source is never used alone: a Tier 1 source must stand beside it.

Tier 3 · Plausible lead

Worth a look, not yet solid

A preprint, a single source, or a claim we couldn't trace to where it started. It is allowed only if the page says why it's included and asks questions to ponder. It never carries an instruction, and nothing else on the site leans on it.

03 · The rules

Justify it, or it doesn't go in.

Required justification

Every case file states, in a sentence, why its claim holds the tier it holds. If we can't write that sentence, the claim isn't published.

Upgrade rules

  • Tier 3 to Tier 2: a reputed outlet independently traces the claim to a primary source.
  • Tier 2 to Tier 1: we open the primary source ourselves and the date and figures match.

Drop rules

  • The source is withdrawn, or a Tier 1 source contradicts it.
  • At a re-check we can't find it again, or can't say who said it first.
  • A number or name doesn't match when we compare it with the original. We drop it and say so in the change log, rather than quietly fixing it.

Instructions, people and examples

  • Health, money and safety instructions are shown only as what an official source says, named and linked. They are never our own advice, and never rest on Tier 2 or 3 alone. mind is not a clinician or a helpline. See Get help now.
  • People. A case file does not make claims about what a named person did. It looks at claims, studies and official records.
  • Real studies carry their authors, year, journal and a link. Invented examples (the voice note, the school, the five messages) stay clearly labelled as invented.

When we re-check

  • The Get help now page: at least every 30 days. Next due by 23 Nov 2026.
  • Case files: at least every 90 days. Next due by 22 Jan 2027.
  • The calendar: listings are pointers and change often. Check the organiser's page before you go.

04 · The card format

Every case file has the same parts.

  1. The claim, in the words people use.
  2. The tier, with a one-sentence reason, and the date it was last checked.
  3. The clues, two columns, each with its source.
  4. The doubts: what we can't tell.
  5. Where the clues point: a leaning, not a verdict.
  6. Questions to ponder.
  7. Sources, each with its own tier and the date we opened it.

See one: Rereading: why does it feel like studying? Or browse all case files.

05 · Corrections

If we're wrong, we say so.

  • Tell us in the comments on any content page. It needs a GitHub account, and comments are public, so please keep personal details out. We don't yet have a route that needs no account.
  • We fix the page, change the "last checked" date, and add a line to the log below. We don't edit silently.
  • If a correction affects a helpline, we fix that first.
