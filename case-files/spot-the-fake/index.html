Case file 06 · all case files

Can you tell a real face from a generated one?

This one you can test on yourself, but the studies tested people for you. Here is what they found.

The claim

I can tell an AI-generated face or video from a real one just by looking.

Tier 1 · Primary Last checked 24 Oct 2026 (IST)

Why this tier: Two peer-reviewed papers opened by DOI (abstracts read), plus the CERT-In advisory and BOOM's own page, which we opened today. What the tiers mean is on how we check.

The clues

What the clues say.

Clues against “I can always tell”

  • Nightingale and Farid (2022): people were 48.2% accurate at telling real faces from generated ones (N = 315, 128 faces). With two choices, guessing gives about 50%. Generated faces were also rated about 7.7% more trustworthy (4.82 against 4.48 on their scale; N = 223).
  • Groh and colleagues (2022): across 15,016 participants, ordinary people were roughly 66–69% accurate on sampled videos. Better than chance, and still wrong about one time in three.

Clues about what helps

  • In the same video study, the leading computer model scored 65% on the full 4,000-video hold-out, and a crowd of people plus the model averaged up to 86%. Combining judgements did better than either alone.
  • CERT-In, India's computer emergency response team, has an advisory (CIAD-2024-0060, “Deepfakes – Threats and Countermeasures”, 27 Nov 2024, severity High). BOOM, a fact-checking outlet, publishes a WhatsApp tipline (+91 77009 06588) where content can be sent for checking.

The doubts

What we can't tell.

  • The faces study used images from one generator (StyleGAN2). Newer generators aren't covered.
  • These are lab tasks with still images or a chosen set of clips. A forwarded video on a phone is a different setting.
  • We have not checked how accuracy changes with compression, language or topic.
  • We cannot say how the BOOM tipline performs. We only know the number is on BOOM's own page.

Where the clues point

Looking alone is not a dependable test for faces, and for video people do better than chance but are often wrong. “I can always tell” isn't supported. Checks that don't depend on your eyes, such as who posted it first and where else it appears, carry more weight. This is a leaning, not a verdict. It changes if the clues do.

Questions to ponder

Over to you.

  1. The last time you called a picture “obviously fake” or “obviously real”, what did you base it on?
  2. If the generated face looks more trustworthy than the real one, what does “looks trustworthy” tell you?
  3. What is one check you could make that doesn't depend on your eyes?
