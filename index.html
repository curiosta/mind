How to learn, and how not to be fooled.

Information is cheap. A finished answer is cheaper. What is scarce is a memory you actually lived, and a claim you can trace back to a person, a date, and a method.

01 · The problem

Two failures, same week.

You ask a model to explain a chapter. It does. You nod. Next morning you cannot rebuild the argument without the screen. That is not learning. That is borrowing.

Someone forwards a voice note. It sounds like a relative. The account is old. The request is urgent. You almost send the money. That is not a new trick. Urgency and a familiar voice are older than writing. The machine only made them cheap.

mind is for both. How to learn when answers are free. How not to be fooled by a machine, or by a person.

02 · The spells

Five skills. The names are a memory aid.

The Patronus

A specific memory, not a mood. If you cannot recall what you saw, you cannot tell it from what was written for you.

Occlumency

Name the feeling before you act. The share button is faster than the thought.

Legilimency

Notice what is repeated, and what is left out. Including in your own reading.

Priori Incantatem

Trace the claim. A quote has a speaker. A number has a table. A photo has a date.

The Foundation

Observation first, model second. If the conclusion arrived before the evidence, you did not build it.

03 · Today

One pass. Ten minutes.

  1. Write one memory from yesterday that a stranger could picture. Smells, words, hands. Not "it was nice."
  2. Pick one claim you almost forwarded. Who said it first? If you cannot name them, do not send it.
  3. Close the tab on something you "already know." Write three lines from memory. The gap is the lesson.
