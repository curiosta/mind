The ideas

Five definitions. Everything else hangs off these.

This page is the spine of the site. The concepts are the practice. The modules are where they get used. These are the ideas the site actually believes, and next to each one is the place where you can feel it for yourself.

One question first
Remembering what you learned, and not falling for a fake. Are those one skill or two?

01 · One skill, two directions

Learning and defence are the same skill.

Anchor memory and tracing the source use the same muscle. Building a memory you can actually recall and tracing a claim back to its source both depend on knowing what you experienced, as opposed to what you were shown. If you can't recall what you saw, you can't tell it apart from what was written for you. If you can't trace a claim, you can't tell it apart from one that was manufactured. It's one skill, pointed inward and outward.

Feel it: the 60-second test, then the tree chain.

02 · The barrier moved

Information used to be scarce. Judgment is scarce now.

When information was hard to reach, access was the product. That's what search engines sold: a way to find what existed. Now information is free and everywhere, so the scarce thing is no longer the document. It's the judgment that decides which document to trust, and whether you still have that judgment when you look away. The site's job isn't to deliver information. It's to build that judgment.

Feel it: the phone-ban article.

03 · The old tricks are real

Old tricks, newly cheap.

Urgency, flattery, repetition, a familiar voice. All older than print. What's changed is the cost of aiming them: a model can write a fresh version for each reader, in that reader's own words. The counter-move is still a learning skill. Name the pull, trace the claim, then decide.

Feel it: the voice note and would you act?

04 · Close the tool

Knowledge is what survives the closed tab.

The tool is allowed to speed up the search. But if the only copy of an idea sits in a chat log, you don't have the idea. You have a transcript, and a transcript doesn't come with you into a conversation, an exam or a decision.

Feel it: which prompt would you type?

05 · Build last

Hutchinson's order: foundation, question, observation, deduction, model.

The order is the belief. Start from what a careful person could see, let that force a question, and only then risk a model. A model that arrives early is someone else's conclusion worn as your own. You understand a model when you can say which new observation would make you give it up.

Feel it: what floats? This site follows the same order. Every page asks you to observe first and gives the model last.

The through-line

The scarce thing was never information.

It's the person who can hold a memory, trace a claim and build a model, in that order. That's what this site is for.
